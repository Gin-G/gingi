## Prompt

Nick pasted this exchange and asked "How should I respond?"

His email:
> Hi Leigh,
> Any day in particular work best for you? I'm typically in the office on Tuesdays and Thursdays. It looks like maybe Tuesday the 14th is open before 10 for both of us?
> Let me know if you have a preference and I'll get something on the calendar.
> Thanks,
> Nick

Leigh's reply:
> Hi there!
> Yes let's aim for that. My house flooded overnight... So my schedule will be a bit unpredictable for the next few weeks.
> -Leigh

## Claude's draft

Oh no — sorry to hear about the flooding, Leigh. That's a rough one. Let's keep the 14th on the calendar for now, but no worries at all if you need to push it while you're getting things sorted — just let me know and we'll find another time. Hope the cleanup goes as smoothly as it can.

— Nick

(Claude also appended a note below the draft offering to "release the date now" instead of keeping the 14th tentatively.)

## My revision

Ugh. That stinks. Best of luck getting everything cleaned up and back in order.

I'll throw something on our calendars and just let me know if you have to move it for any reason.
-Nick

## What changed

- Empathy as short fragments, not a composed sentence. "Ugh. That stinks." replaced "Oh no — sorry to hear about the flooding, Leigh. That's a rough one." In casual email Nick reacts in clipped two-word fragments; he doesn't build a polished sympathy sentence.
- Don't restate what the recipient already told you. Claude re-named "the flooding" and addressed "Leigh" by name; Nick did neither — she knows what happened and who she is. Cut the recap.
- Personal beat grouped together, logistics last. Nick's order is [reaction + well-wish], then [calendar]. Claude split the well-wish off to the very end, after the logistics.
- Dropped the false-specific "the 14th ... for now." Nick wrote "I'll throw something on our calendars" — no date restated, no "for now" hedge. The date was already settled earlier in the thread; repeating it adds nothing.
- Let a plain statement carry the flexibility instead of cushioning it. Claude padded the out with "no worries at all," "while you're getting things sorted," and "we'll find another time." Nick trusts one clause: "just let me know if you have to move it for any reason."
- Casual verb, no em-dashes. "throw something on our calendars"; short declarative sentences and periods rather than em-dash asides.
- The hedging note was unnecessary. Claude surfaced a keep-vs-release question below the draft; Nick resolved it inside the message by simply committing to put something down with an easy out. When a scheduling out is this routine, draft it — don't elevate it to a decision for Nick.
