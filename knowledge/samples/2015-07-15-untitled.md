Haha, that made me laugh, obviously this is someone who has much more Lulu
experience than me, so I feel like they are pretty jaded by a bunch of LL's
bullshit, but I can definitely see some parallels.

I don't work full time, so I'm not sure about the lack of benefits that
they talk about, although I do remember from training that one of our
manager's said there was no 401k, (which even Mike got as a bus driver from
TOV). I did find it funny recently that although they hired a bunch of us
part time a month ago, they just started an interview process for more part
time this past week. I'm sure that is because LL doesn't want to pay all
that's entailed for full time staff (full time gets a bigger discount on
clothes and higher hourly), and they put all of us on seasonal contracts,
even though many of us have said we want to work past the end date and
would be happy to go full time, probably for the same reason, and so they
don't have to pay benefits when our contract ends. The overtime thing is
very true as well. It's weird, every job I have worked at you work until
everything is done; at LL, you NEED to clock out at the time you are
scheduled until, even if you are closing, and the morning shift will pick
up your slack. So much for personal responsibility.

The pay part was the only thing I didn't really see eye to eye on her with,
mostly because we get a flat rate of $14/hr, although most of my paychecks
are $16/hr, and then generous bonus program they hand out gave me $20/hr
this past pay period (this hourly is unique to Vail, as they try to pay
proportionate to cost of living in the area). Not a ton of money, but the
only place you will make more is if you were to go to high end retail, like
Gorsuch or Axel's, and make commission. Most places in Vail don't pay that
much, so I have no complaints.

The store is peppered with libertarian propaganda, which I think is funny
because so many of the management (who are SO into Lulu to the point that
it's hard to voice a differing opinion) probably don't agree with the
manifesto of Ayn Rand, nor have they read any of her books, although Atlas
Shrugged, which I have read, is both on the floor with some Colorado
mountaineering books, and in the back room. We also had to listen to a
motivational speaker via youtube before we started named Brian Tracy.
Although he had some pretty interesting points that I took to heart about
goal setting, it was some pretty conservative philosophy that, to be
honest, I think a lot of my peers there are probably a little too naive to
pick up on. But that is kind of my bigger problem with Lululemon; they
frame the job description as one that is empowering and full of
responsibility, when in fact, every aspect of a shift is so micromanaged
that you get chewed out if you leave the section you are scheduled in.

The part about feedback cracked me up too; everything at the store is some
sort of "awesome positive feedback" that's meant to be a learning lesson,
although a "hey, next time do it this way" would have probably sufficed.

I feel like I could have gotten drunk with the author of this article and
done some serious hating on Lululemon. Sorry for my rant of a response,
pretty funny article and got the wheels turning!

Tell Carolyn and Samson I say hi, it was nice to see you guys the other
week, hope we get to do it again soon!
