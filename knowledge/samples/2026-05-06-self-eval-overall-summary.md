I had a solid fiscal year and was able to get a lot accomplished. This is the first full fiscal year that CIRRUS ran as a production cluster. We onboarded over 20 different user applications and connected CIRRUS as a GitHub Actions runner to over 2 dozen repositories which was a lot more than I had hoped and planned for. As part of socializing CIRRUS capabilities I led numerous presentations on the platform as well as created a dedicated site to showcase the different user workload examples running on the cluster. Continued outreach and growth will be a top priority for me for years to come and I was impressed by the traction I saw CIRRUS have after one year. Seeing others reference using CIRRUS in their presentations at different conferences and talks, like the Improving Scientific Software conference, showcases the initial success and possibilities for the future. Near the beginning of the fiscal year we migrated the GDEX platform off VMs run by NRIT and on to CIRRUS. This involved database migrations, DNS updates and strategies, and containerizing the old RDA website so it could be rebranded as GDEX on CIRRUS. During the migration I had to unexpectedly leave early for parental leave. The teams involved picked up the migration without missing a step and hit our road map deadlines. I had been handling a number of technical tasks for the migration, and the teams had the knowledge and experience to take this on while I was gone. I was the most proud of this as it showcases how my leadership style empowers the team to accomplish new tasks without needing their hand held. I also completed LEAD training and was deliberate in introducing topics learned in to my one on one and team meetings to become a better listener and get more value out of those meeting. The growth and progress I have seen in the teams I work with, and the CIRRUS workloads in general, has exceeded my expectations of what I thought we could accomplish this fiscal year.
