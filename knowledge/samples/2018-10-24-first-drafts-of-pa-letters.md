Why you want to attend, specifics about MCPHS and Worchester area

I received my Bachelor’s degree from the University of Colorado in Boulder.
I was also born, and raised, in Boulder Colorado. My attraction to MCPHS,
specifically the Worcester campus, is most intriguing because I would be
exposed to an area that is different than what I am used to. The
opportunity to expand my exposure to a diverse array of individual
patients, cultures, and lifestyles that are different from what I have
experienced in Colorado is very attractive. The Worcester area offers a
very diverse population, and being able to perform clinical studies
throughout greater Boston allows me to grow my experiences in a meaningful
way.

            I’m attracted to the Worcester campus for a variety of reasons.
Although Worcester is considered a metropolitan city, based on my visits to
the area, I see a balance between suburn, rural, natural, and city
environments. This variety increases the amount of diversity seen in this
area, making it a better sampling of the general worldwide population than
other areas may offer. The geographical differences from what I have grown
accustomed to in Colorado also are an added benefit. I still am able to
enjoy my pastimes, such as snowboarding and hiking, but in a completely
different environment. Worcester is also attractive as I do have a large
family support system located nearby. Although it isn’t necessary for
success, I believe that this support only strengthens my ability to be
successful, which is quite high already.

            I am interested in an accelerated program as I have a very
strong desire to learn, and am excited for the opportunity to jump in
headfirst. From a classroom perspective MCPHS Worcester offers what I am
looking for in an education and more. It is important to me that my
professors have a clinical background, and still practice clinically. You
can get out of touch in this profession quickly if you are not out in the
field. I crave exposure to all there is to offer at MCPHS, the classroom
environment, collaboration with my fellow classmates, simulations in the
lab, preparing for the unexpected, my own personnel learning experience,
but more so the ability to improve my working with a team. It was a team of
medical professionals that saved my moms life multiple times that led me
down this path. While she ultimately succumbed to her battle with
Neuro-endicrine cancer, the experiences that I was part of, and witnessed,
have driven me to become a part of that medical team. I have craved
experience in as many different areas of the medical profession, and have
branched out as much as I have been able to. Although my ultimate goal is
to become a practicing PA, I will continue to fill roles in the medical
team as much as I can. Expanding my knowledge of as many roles allows me to
better serve each member of the teams I am a part of by understanding
motivations, and demands shared across different professions that make up a
highly productive medical team.

            As the medical profession continues to grow, the PA profession
will become more and more integral in delivering excellent primary care to
patients. MCPHS understands the demands in the field and how the landscape
is ever evolving. Delivering PA training for over 20 years will force you
to be in tune with the needs of the community and MCPHS has shown that is
ready and willing to grow and pivot to meet those needs. Offering a pathway
in to a Doctorate of Physicians Assistant is the way of the future, and
although I am not at that stage in my career, seeing a progressive program
acknowledge that the way of the future is through more highly trained
primary care professionals is very encouraging. I would very much like an
opportunity to succeed in an environment that is diversely different from
what I am used to, acknowledges that evolution is an actuality every day in
the medical field, encourages hands on opportunities that I am able to take
advantage of to drive my success, and is closely engrained in to
surrounding area.

Please describe a stressful experience and its impact on your life

The most stressful experience I have had to endure so far in my life was my
mothers illness, and eventual passing. She was diagnosed with
Neuroendicrine cancer that eventually led to a double adrenalectomy and
many other side effects while I was attending front range community
college.

Initially this impacted my life in a day to day, time management, aspect
mostly. I had to learn how to manage my studies, visiting my mother in the
facility she was in that day, and still be able to maintain social
relationships. I attended all my classes, got all my school work done,
visited her for at least an hour, and was able to spend a little time with
friends during the beginning stages of her illness. I performed admirably
in my academic life making the Deans list every semester I had to juggle
everything. I was also able to address my social needs and it was during
this team that a relationship with a friend evolved in to what eventually
would become my husband. I had no options during this time, I had to get
everything done, care for my mom, and still take care of my own mental well
being. The time management skills that were constructed during this time
have followed me throughout my life.

After the initial impact, I developed a new passion while observing the
team of medical professionals that were taking care of my mother during her
illness. This passion could not be ignored, and I changed my major from
Psychology to Science, one that focused on anatomy and physiology. Seeing
the team operate together in a seamless way provided all the motivation I
needed to spark my interest in becoming a member of that team.

The other aspect of my mothers illness that impacted my life was my lack of
medical knowledge as she entered in to a variety of treatments. I became
fascinated in the what, why, and how of the human body. I have favorite
subjects during my education so far, Endicronolgy stands out for obvious
reasons, but I was fascinated by all of them. Any reservations I may have
had about whether my decision to pursue health care were correct left me
after my 1st class at CU Boulder. Months after my mother had passed, I
stood outside a door leading to a cadaver lab. Worry about mental toughness
in this environment so shortly after my mothers passing was gone as
fascination quickly overcame any lingering doubts.

Faced with an opportunity to shut down, and be complacent, I went the
opposite way and excelled. I am defined by so much more than my moms
illness at this point in my life, but it was that experience that helped
shape me in to the person I have become today. Resillient, curious, hard
working, persistant, compassionate, and really too many more adjectives
that describe how I have shaped who I am and want to be.

Please explain why you are a good match for the PA profession

            I am a good match for the PA profession for a number of
reasons, most of which have been determined through personal experience. I
believe an exceptional practicing PA requires a variety of learned and
innate qualities. These include, but are not limited to, problem solving,
professionalism, communication, mental and emotional fortitude, patient
care, and medical knowledge. Not all of these are able to be taught, or
learned, in a classroom. The concepts can be implemented and best practices
can be followed, but it is not a natural thing for everyone. I have all the
skills necessary to be a successful practitioner.

My personal experiences have helped shaped my approach to patient care. My
mother passed away when I was 23 after a long battle with cancer. Everyone
of my patients deserves to be treated the way I would treat my own mother,
and I practice that without question or interruption. No matter what level
of care is being administered there is an appropriate way to interact with
patients, and their families, irregardless of any difficulties that may be
encountered in those interactions. Professionalism is one of the core
values that I implement every day whether I am practing or not.

            Mental and emotional strength is something that is often tested
in any medical profession. There have been numerous times when this has
been tested in my career so far. I have a difficult time seeing children
mistreat their parents after losing one of my own. It takes mental
integrity to remain professional in all of these instances, and it is not
something I am willing to sacrifice in order to prove a point to myself. I
am often reminded of my mom during interactions with patients, and have
never let it cloud my judgement, or impair my level of care.

            My level of care is impacted directly by the amount of medical
knowledge I have, and the certifications I posses to backup that knowledge.
I am fascinated by the human body, and it has become a passion to explore
and learn as many different concepts as I can. My thirst for this knowledge
has not been quenched, and it will continue to drive me in any medical
career I pursue.

            I am not without my faults and understanding what you know and
what you don’t know is a necessity when you are a practicing PA. I know
that I do not want the responsibility of a Physician, or the ….

I do know that I want to be involved with the diagnosis, treatment, and
prescription of medicine in a team environment. My specific skill set is
best suited for a Physician Assistant. I know that I will not fail in my
pursuit of this goal, and that anyone who gives me a chance to prove it
will get the best candidate they will ever see.
