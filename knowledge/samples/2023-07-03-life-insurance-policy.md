We initially were going to move forward with the $850,000 of total coverage between the whole life insurance and term insurance.

I know you would prefer to invest the difference, but I would consider the benefits of the whole life insurance when deciding. Below are a few key points.

  *   Whole life insurance death benefit is tax free upon your death, where an investment account will be taxable to your beneficiaries and must (IRS rules) be liquidated within 10 years of inheriting.
     *   For example, if your beneficiaries (other then your wife) were to inherit $300,000 in an IRA they would have 10 years to liquidate that account and claim that $300,000 as income. That will ultimately raise tax brackets for them leading to lesser net income.
        *   Not a bad problem to have but certainly a thing to consider.
     *   Whole life insurance will add a fixed bucket of money to your overall retirement strategy.
        *   Let’s say you retired in January of 2022; would you have a bucket of money to pull from that would not have lost the 15% last year in the markets? In an instance like last year, you would have not wanted to pull income from your security investments (stocks, bonds, mutual funds etc.) and let them recover. Having a fixed bucket of money in down markets can make all the difference of not only running out money in retirement but also leaving a bigger inheritance behind for your family.
           *   History of the Whole life policies shows that you should ideally gross nearly 6%.
     *   Whole life insurance can be accessed prior to 59 ½ unlike most investments.
     *   Whole life insurance cash value can be withdrawn tax free while not being claimed on your taxes as income.
