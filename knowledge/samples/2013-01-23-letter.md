Dear President Obama,

I recently graduated from college with a degree in Mechanical Engineering.
This is one of the paths you have been preaching to American students.
 Pursue an education in Science and Technology and help get this country
back on top. I ended up getting a great job straight out of college and am
now making more money than the average American household. I am on the path
that you yourself have been preaching and still find myself facing
financial hardships.  With rent, groceries, gas, utilities, insurance, and
student loan payments I am barely able to save any money let alone spend it
on luxury items.  How am I supposed to help stimulate the economy when I
can’t even save money to purchase a home or support a family? When I make
more money than the average household how is the average American expected
to do this?

Greed has driven the economy to our current predicament. I am sickened at
how every American is nickel and dimed in order to maximize profits while
we get inferior products. This country grew to power on a simple premise;
create products with longevity and pride. Now we cut corners, use inferior
materials, stuff our food with chemicals, create too much waste, while all
the savings are not passed on to the consumer. Instead we pay a premium for
a product that will most likely break or contain something that gives us
cancer.  You come across as an average man, besides the fact that you are
the President. Your family is the most important thing to you, you enjoy
watching sports, and you enjoy a cold adult beverage every once in a while.
That being said I wonder if you have ever asked yourself a question I think
about when I go out for diner. Why does it cost five dollars or more for a
“cheap” beer at some restaurants?  It feels like I am being robbed, I can
buy the same one for 75 cents at the liquor store.  This is one example of
how we are overcharged for so many products in order to maximize profits
and this is done almost everywhere I look. The extent that the American
population is ripped off and overcharged is borderline criminal.

Waste is almost a bigger problem than greed. We waste obscene amounts of
money on things we think are important.  Billions of dollars are spent on
advertising products that don’t need to be advertised. Why does Coke or
Pepsi need to advertise at all? Most gas stations only carry one or the
other, forcing people to buy what is carried, making the advertisements
useless. People will buy their products with or without commercials. A
McDonald’s commercial will do less than the physical McDonald’s buildings
that are everywhere.  Sometimes advertising has the opposite effect,
especially when it’s jammed down your throat.  I had always planned on
voting for you in the 2012 election. I was bombarded with phone calls,
e-mails, snail mail, as well as endless radio and television commercials
that cost billions of dollars. It was annoying me so much that I almost
changed my vote.  The American population has been so over run by
advertisements that we chose to ignore them most of the time.

I often walk through the grocery store and look at the tons and tons of
food stored there.  How much of it will be thrown away when it goes bad?
It’s deplorable in just one store, and then I think about how many stores
in the country have the same amount of waste.  How are we going to sustain
our population if we keep living like this, keep dumping plastic in the
oceans and trash anywhere we can get it out of the way, keep valuing
celebrity and sports news over real news, and more importantly placing our
values in the complete wrong places? I used to get angry over this, and
then I realized it is my fault. It is every single Americans fault.  No
matter how many good things we are doing there is something else we can do
better.  People look to you in order to figure out who is to blame for all
our problems recently.  Some don’t even look to you, instead they just
blame you.  I know who to place the blame on but no one cares what I have
to say. You have the power. To put it bluntly you should publically place
the blame in every American, including yourself.  If we collectively, as a
Nation, want to change the way things are we have that power. One man can’t
solve our problems no matter how much influence or support he has. It takes
each individual, doing their part, to change what has become bad back to
good.  We could use some help regaining our faith in the government. This
could be accomplished by cutting us slack in a lot of different areas. Did
you know that if I get approved for too many credit cards and loans and
don’t pay them off by the time I die my next of kin gets stuck with the
bill? Of course you know but do you find that as criminal as I do?  In my
opinion that was bad business by the bank. Companies that practice bad
business usually don’t last. They end up staying in business by passing off
the debt from one person to another.

The main reason I wrote you is because I think my student interest rates
are too high.  I’ll pay back twice what I borrowed if I only make my
minimum payment. Obviously I think that there are a lot of other problems
in this country, most of them are very fixable.   We created the economy,
currency, business ethics and practices, Presidency, loans, credit, and
almost anything else you can think of. These are not natural issues, they
are manmade. The way we go about things is not correct. There is more than
likely a way to solve all our problems. It most likely involves the use of
money.  The greedy are unwilling to part with it or it is being wasted on
things that are illogical.  If we stopped cutting corners to save money and
increase profits we could create more jobs.  Some of our processes have
become so efficient we over produce, again to sell more and maximize
profits.
